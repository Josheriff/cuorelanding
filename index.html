When generic stops working

Your systems should remove friction, not create more of it.

Custom development becomes worth considering when your teams repeatedly have to work around the software:

  • Critical work still happens in spreadsheets
  • Teams re-enter the same data across systems
  • Approvals and handoffs depend on manual follow-up
  • Per-seat and add-on costs keep growing
  • Integrations are fragile or incomplete
  • Modules you pay for and never open

The problem isn’t your business. The problem is software that wasn’t built for it.

FAQ

Questions we hear often.

What is custom ERP development?

Designing and building the software that runs your core operations — records, documents, workflows, approvals, integrations and reporting — around how your company actually works, instead of reshaping your processes to fit a packaged platform’s limits.

Do you sell an ERP product?

No. We sell a senior-led development service. There is no Cuore Tech ERP license to subscribe to, and no two systems we build are the same: each one starts from your processes, data and integrations, and contains only the modules you asked for.

How is this different from an established ERP platform?

A packaged platform gives every customer the same core product and asks you to configure around it. Custom work starts from your workflow: your documents, stages, approvals and integrations are the product rather than exceptions bolted onto someone else’s roadmap. For many companies a well-configured platform is the better answer, and we say so.

Is custom ERP software expensive?

Scope determines cost. Projects typically start at $10,000, and the first phase focuses on the highest-value module. A wider system covering finance, inventory, production and payroll is a larger program, and we scope it in phases rather than promising a complete platform for the entry price.

Can you replace our existing system?

Often, yes — depending on what the current system holds and how accessible that data is. We map the data and workflows worth keeping, plan a controlled transition, and build around your real process instead of recreating the old system’s structure.

Can it integrate with the tools we already use?

Usually, subject to available APIs and technical feasibility. Typical integrations include accounting and finance platforms, payment providers, payroll, email and calendars, e-commerce and POS, and internal databases. We integrate those where an existing product already does the job well.

Where does the software run, and who owns it?

On infrastructure you control or managed hosting you choose. The hosting, source-code and ownership model is agreed in the project scope, so your data and your roadmap stay under your direction rather than a vendor’s.

Do you add AI to the software?

Where it removes real work: data entry, document handling, classification and search across your records. It is also how our own engineers move faster. Architecture, security, data modeling and product decisions stay with senior engineers, and anything automated stays under human review.

What happens in the free fit review?

We spend about 30 minutes on your current setup, bottlenecks, integrations and goals. You get an honest build-or-buy recommendation, what we would solve first and a likely approach. There is no obligation — and if a packaged platform is the better answer, we will tell you.

Who hosts and maintains the system after go-live?

Your choice. Some companies run what we build in their own cloud account; others prefer managed hosting with support. Hosting, maintenance, updates, backups and support responsibilities are set out in the scope before development starts, and the source code and documentation stay yours as agreed.

Do you build accounting or payroll?

Usually not from scratch. We integrate the accounting, payroll and banking platforms you already use and build the operational data flow into them. If a finance module is genuinely required, we scope it separately.

Do you work with our existing developers or IT team?

Yes. We can own delivery end to end, work alongside an internal team, or hand over a documented system with the guardrails your team needs to maintain it.
